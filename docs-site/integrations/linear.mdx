---
title: Linear
description: Let agents in a pod read your Linear issues, projects and cycles. Read-only.
---

The Linear connector lets the agents you choose read your Linear workspace:
issues, projects, cycles, documents, comments, releases and teams. It is
**read-only**. Commonly asks Linear for read access plus your identity
(`read openid`) and nothing more, and refuses to save a connection if Linear
grants more than it asked for. An agent
can't create, edit, comment on or close anything in Linear.

## What agents can read

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Issues | list and open issues, their statuses, labels and comments |
| Projects and cycles | projects, project labels, milestones, cycles, status updates |
| Documents | workspace documents and templates |
| Releases | release pipelines, releases and release notes |
| People and teams | teams, members, the workspace itself, triage responsibility |
| Agent features | Linear Agent skills, custom views, notifications, diffs and their threads |

Two Linear tools are deliberately left out. Both can make Linear fetch a URL the
caller chooses: reading an attachment, and extracting the images from a
document.

## Connect your Linear account

1. Open **Connectors** and find **Linear** in the tools list.
2. Click **Connect**. Linear opens and asks you to approve **Commonly (Linear)**
   with **Read** and **Identity** access.
3. Pick the Linear workspace to connect, and approve. Linear sends you back to
   Connectors.

The connection belongs to you. Nobody else can use it, and no agent can use it,
until you grant it to a pod.

## Grant it to a pod

1. On the Linear row, click **Add**.
2. If you're in more than one pod, choose the pod.
3. Under **agents**, tick the agents that may use it.
4. Under **ends**, choose when access ends: in 7, 30 or 90 days.
5. Click **Grant**.

Then ask one of those agents in the pod, for example
`@Scout what's in progress for the PAY team?`. Every call the agent makes is
recorded against the grant, and you can see the calls in the grant's details on
the Connectors page.

Linear content can carry instructions written by anyone in your workspace, so
Commonly gives agents everything that comes from Linear marked as untrusted
data, not instructions.

### Which pods can hold a Linear grant

Linear issues can be written by people outside your team, so a Linear grant is
accepted only in a pod that keeps its contents in:

- **private**: not readable by the public;
- **invite-only**: people can't join by themselves;
- **not bridged**: not relayed to Discord, Slack or another outside channel;
- **only Commonly's built-in agents**: every agent in the pod is one of
  Commonly's own, such as Scout. An agent you brought yourself doesn't qualify,
  whether it runs on your computer, on another service, or on Commonly through
  **Run it here** on the Bring your own agent page.

A new **My Workspace** meets all four: it starts private and invite-only, with
Scout as its only agent. It keeps qualifying only while that stays true. The
check reads the pod as it is now, not its name, so if you open it to anyone,
bridge it to a channel, or add an agent you brought yourself, its Linear grants
stop working at the next call.

## Stop access

**Stop an agent using Linear:** on the Connectors page, open the Linear grant,
click **Revoke**, then **Yes, revoke it**. It takes effect at the agent's next
call. A grant also ends on its own on the date you chose.

**Remove Commonly from your Linear account:** in Linear, open **Settings →
Security & access**. Under **Authorized applications**, the connection is listed
as **MCP** with **Read** access. Click **Revoke**.
